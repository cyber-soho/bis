Enterprise Information Systems & Strategic IT

Flashcards

Click on any card to flip it and reveal the definition.

Information System (IS)
A system that collects, stores, processes, and shares information to help people make decisions.
Enterprise Information System (EIS)
An IS that serves a whole organisation at once, ensuring every department works from the same integrated data.
Defining characteristic of an "Enterprise" system
Integration. It relies on one shared source of truth instead of many private, disconnected copies of data.
Six building blocks of an Information System
Hardware, Software, Data, Networks (Telecommunications), People, and Processes.
The two crucial building blocks that determine if IT pays off
People and Processes. Without trained staff and agreed-upon procedures, even the best technology will fail.
Transaction Processing System (TPS)
The base layer of the IS pyramid serving front-line operations by capturing individual events (e.g., "Did this sale go through?").
Management Information System (MIS)
The layer serving middle managers that summarizes TPS data to answer operational questions like "How did the store do this week?".
Decision Support System (DSS)
The layer serving analysts and managers, used to model scenarios and answer "what-if" questions.
Executive Support System (ESS)
The top layer of the IS pyramid serving senior executives to guide long-term strategy (e.g., "Where should the company go next?").
Data Silos
A problem where each department keeps its own private copy of data, leading to conflicting records and decisions based on stale numbers.
Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)
An enterprise application that pulls together finance, operations, inventory, and HR into one shared record.
Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
An enterprise application that tracks and integrates every customer interaction across sales and support.
Supply Chain Management (SCM)
An enterprise application that coordinates operations between a company, its suppliers, logistics, and delivery networks.
Knowledge Management System (KMS)
An enterprise application used to store and share the organisation's documents, know-how, and best practices.
When IT becomes "Strategic"
The moment it changes what the business can do that its rivals cannot, moving beyond just being a commodity like electricity.
Porter's Five Forces
A framework to diagnose industry profitability based on Competitive Rivalry, New Entrants, Substitutes, Suppliers, and Buyers.
IT lever against Bargaining Power of Buyers
Using personalisation and lock-in mechanisms to raise switching costs for customers.
IT lever against Threat of New Entrants
Utilizing scale and a strong technological platform to raise the barrier to entry.
Primary Activities in Porter's Value Chain
Inbound logistics, Operations, Outbound logistics, Marketing & Sales, and Service.
Support Activities in Porter's Value Chain
Firm infrastructure, HR management, Technology development, and Procurement.
Formula for Margin in the Value Chain
\(\text{Margin} = \text{Value} - \text{Cost}\), where value is what the customer is willing to pay.
Goal of strategic IT in the Value Chain
To widen the margin by raising value (better products/services) and lowering costs (automation) at the same time.
Payback Period
The amount of time it takes until the cumulative savings from an IT project repay the original investment.
Formula for Return On Investment (ROI)
\(\text{ROI } \% = (\text{Net gain} \div \text{Amount invested}) \times 100\)
Difference between Payback and ROI
Payback tells you *how soon* you break even (risk metric), whereas ROI tells you *how much* you gain (profit metric).

Practice Quiz

1. A retail chain spends millions on a new inventory tracking app. The software is bug-free and the tablets are fast, but store managers keep recording stock on paper out of habit, making the digital data useless. Which building block of an Information System represents the primary point of failure here?
Hint: Consider the difference between the technology itself and how the business organizes human behavior around it.
  • Software
  • Hardware
  • Data
  • Processes
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: Processes
The agreed-upon daily steps and procedures for recording inventory were not adapted or enforced to align with the new technology. (Software and hardware are explicitly stated as working, and bad data is merely a symptom).
2. An analyst at a major airline uses specialized software to simulate how a 10% increase in jet fuel prices might affect route profitability over the next year. Which layer of the IS pyramid are they utilizing?
Hint: Focus on the user's analytical role and the "what-if" nature of the scenario being tested.
  • Transaction Processing System (TPS)
  • Management Information System (MIS)
  • Decision Support System (DSS)
  • Executive Support System (ESS)
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: Decision Support System (DSS)
This layer provides analytical tools specifically designed to model scenarios and answer "what-if" questions involving changing variables, unlike TPS (transactions) or MIS (historical summaries).
3. A customer calls support to complain about a delayed delivery. The support agent sees the order status as "Processing", but the warehouse system already marked it "Shipped" two days ago. What structural problem is causing this discrepancy?
Hint: Think about what happens to truth when different departments maintain their own separate, unlinked databases.
  • The company relies on data silos.
  • The Transaction Processing System (TPS) failed.
  • The Executive Support System (ESS) lacks operational visibility.
  • CRM software inherently processes slower than SCM software.
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: The company relies on data silos.
When departments maintain private, disconnected copies of records, updates in one area fail to reflect in others, leading to conflicting truths.
4. A streaming service creates a highly personalized recommendation algorithm that takes years to learn a user's exact preferences. If the user cancels and joins a rival service, they must start training a new algorithm from scratch. Which of Porter's Five Forces is this IT strategy primarily designed to weaken?
Hint: Consider who holds the power when a customer can easily leave, and how losing personalized data makes leaving painful.
  • Threat of New Entrants
  • Bargaining Power of Buyers
  • Bargaining Power of Suppliers
  • Threat of Substitutes
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: Bargaining Power of Buyers
By tying deep personalization to the user's history, the company artificially raises switching costs, making it harder and less appealing for the customer to exercise their power to leave.
5. A logistics firm deploys an AI system that automatically calculates the most fuel-efficient delivery routes in real-time, significantly cutting down diesel expenses for its fleet. In which primary activity of Porter's Value Chain is IT adding value here?
Hint: Review the definitions of Porter's Primary Activities and identify which one deals directly with moving the finished product out to the end user.
  • Inbound logistics
  • Operations
  • Outbound logistics
  • Service
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: Outbound logistics
This activity is specifically concerned with the physical distribution and delivery of final goods to the customer, which is where the delivery fleet is operating.
6. A bakery invests $30,000 in an automated ordering system. The system saves $12,000 a year in reduced waste, but costs $2,000 a year in software licensing to run. What is the payback period for this investment?
Hint: First calculate the net yearly gain by subtracting the annual running cost from the annual savings, then see how long it takes that net gain to cover the initial investment.
  • 2.5 years
  • 3.0 years
  • 15.0 years
  • 5.0 years
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: 3.0 years
The net yearly gain is $10,000 ($12,000 savings - $2,000 cost). Dividing the $30,000 investment by this $10,000 net gain yields exactly 3 years to break even.
7. A multinational consulting firm realizes its junior analysts are spending hours reinventing research methodologies that senior partners have already perfected but kept hidden in personal hard drives. Which enterprise application should the firm implement to capture and share this expertise globally?
Hint: Look for the application category designed specifically to capture, store, and distribute human expertise and internal documents.
  • Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
  • Supply Chain Management (SCM)
  • Knowledge Management System (KMS)
  • Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: Knowledge Management System (KMS)
This system is built expressly to centralize an organization's know-how, documents, and best practices into a shared enterprise resource, solving the issue of hidden methodologies.
8. Every Friday afternoon, a regional director receives a generated report showing the total sales volume across all ten stores in their territory for that week, compared against the previous week. Which system layer is generating this summary report?
Hint: Consider the management level of the user and the summarized, historical nature of the weekly data provided.
  • Transaction Processing System (TPS)
  • Management Information System (MIS)
  • Decision Support System (DSS)
  • Executive Support System (ESS)
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: Management Information System (MIS)
This layer is designed for middle managers, aggregating granular transaction data (from the TPS) into reports that answer operational questions over a set timeframe.
9. A luxury hotel introduces a mobile app that allows guests to bypass the front desk and unlock their rooms with their phones. This reduces front desk staffing costs and simultaneously increases guest satisfaction by eliminating wait times. According to the Value Chain framework, what is the net effect on the hotel's margin?
Hint: Recall the formula for Margin, and consider how a single strategic technology can push the two variables in opposite, favorable directions.
  • Margin remains unchanged because the app's development cost offsets the staffing savings.
  • Margin narrows because increasing customer value always requires proportionally higher costs.
  • Margin widens because the technology simultaneously raises value and lowers operational cost.
  • Margin becomes irrelevant because the check-in service is now fully digitized.
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: Margin widens because the technology simultaneously raises value and lowers operational cost.
The app reduces operational expenses while elevating the value the customer receives through convenience, positively impacting both variables in the Margin = Value - Cost formula.
10. A marketing agency spends $50,000 on a new analytics tool. Over a 3-year period, the tool generates a net gain of $60,000. What is the 3-year Return On Investment (ROI) percentage for this project?
Hint: Divide the total net gain by the original investment amount, then multiply by 100 to convert that ratio into a percentage.
  • 120%
  • 83.3%
  • 20%
  • 110%
Show Answer & Rationale
Correct Answer: 120%
Dividing the $60,000 net gain by the $50,000 investment yields 1.2, which multiplied by 100 correctly translates to a 120% return on the investment.
