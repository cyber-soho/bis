What you are practising, and why it matters

Two ideas run through these two days: (1) getting the system in and surviving the project, and (2) the shape of the software and how you get hold of it.

Topic 1 — Getting the system in

How do you switch a company from its old way of working to a new EIS (Enterprise Information System — company-wide software that ties every department together on one shared pool of data)? Which go-live method do you pick, and how do you spot the risks that sink these projects?

🔗 The Standish Group (CHAOS research) — decades of data on why big software projects blow budgets, miss deadlines, or fail to deliver.

Topic 2 — The shape of the software

Why does a well-built EIS end up mirroring the shape of the company itself? And how has the way you buy and run these systems changed — from on-prem servers, to cloud, to composable AI-assisted building blocks?

By the end you'll produce a compact implementation-and-project-management brief backed by a risk register, an architecture sketch, and a five-year cost comparison.

☕ Your company for the day: Northern Roast Coffee Co.

Snapshot. A coffee roaster with one roastery and three cafés, about 40 staff. Today they run the whole business on spreadsheets, a shoebox of supplier invoices, and the memory of office manager Dana.

  • 💸 Duplicate typing & chasing costs roughly $3,000/month in wasted time.
  • 🛑 Owners are cautious: "we cannot afford a disaster during business hours."
  • 📆 There is no hard deadline — decision quality matters more than speed.
Want to bring your own company instead? Go for it — swap in any small-to-mid organisation (bakery, bike shop, clinic, game studio) and use it consistently across all three parts.

📌 Part A · Implementation methods, risk & CSFs Weight 1 · ~50 min

Step A1 — Go-live decision matrix

Big Bang
Everyone switches on one date. Fastest & cheapest; scariest, no safety net.
Phased
Turn on one module at a time. Calmer, longer, pricier, temporary bridges.
Parallel
Old & new run side by side until trusted. Safest; most exhausting/expensive.
Pilot
Go live at one site first, then roll out. Middle ground; shines with many sites.

💡 A cautious, no-deadline company usually lands on Parallel or Phased; a single-site shop under time pressure can justify Big Bang. Deliverable A1: completed matrix + 120–180 word justification naming the winner, tying it to real constraints, and admitting one honest downside.

🌟 Stretch A4 — The pre-mortem (optional)

Imagine it's a year from now and the project failed spectacularly. Write the five-line story of how it went wrong, then mine it for missed risks.

📈 Part B · EIS structure & provisioning Weight 1 · ~50 min

Step B1 — Module map & three-tier architecture

Choose 5–7 modules and tag each to a department (Finance, Sales & CRM, Inventory/WMS, Procurement, HR). Draw the presentation tier (screens), application tier (modules), and database tier (one shared database).

Step B2 — Trace one business process

Pick Order-to-Cash or Procure-to-Pay. Show it crossing ≥3 modules, all reading/writing the same shared database, with one marked failure point.

🌟 Stretch B4 — A modern twist (optional)

Pick an AI/ML demand-forecast, IoT sensor, API link, or low-code tool. Four-line pitch: what it does, which module it touches, what it saves.

🎓 Part C · The integrative brief Weight 3 · ~2h05

This is the main event: fold Parts A and B into one coherent brief a busy owner could read and act on.

C1 · Scope statement
Will / will-not deliver + assumptions. Best defence against scope creep.
C2 · WBS
Discovery → Data migration → Configuration → Training → Go-live → Support. ≥3 phases, 2–4 packages each.
C3 · Timeline/Gantt
Matches your Part A go-live method. ≥4 milestones (e.g. data migrated, pilot live, all sites live, old system retired).
C4 · RACI matrix
6–10 activities × owner/Dana/managers/vendor/IT — exactly one "A" per row.
C5 · Risk & governance
Top 6 risks from Part A + who approves scope/budget changes.
C6 · Provisioning + numbers
TCO summary in owner-friendly cost-benefit language, not IT jargon.
C7 · Executive summary / pitch

One page (or 5–8 slides) an owner reads alone: recommended go-live method & why, headline risks & containment, provisioning choice & 5-year cost, 3–4 milestones, and "what we need from you to start."

🌟 C8 — Your own idea (optional, strongly encouraged)

One page/slide proposing a modern enhancement (AI forecast, IoT sensor, API integration, low-code helper) and its payoff.
