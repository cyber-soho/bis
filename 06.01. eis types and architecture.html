The Foundations of Enterprise Systems

This section introduces the core concepts of Enterprise Information Systems (EIS). You will explore the historical evolution of business software—from paper formulas to cloud-based AI—and interact with the Information Pyramid, a critical framework illustrating how raw, day-to-day transaction data is refined upwards into high-level executive decisions. By clicking through these models, you'll understand why systems exist and who uses them.

Key Distinction: IT vs. IS

IT (Information Technology): The gear and plumbing (computers, networks, cables).

IS (Information System): The whole arrangement (technology + people + data + processes).

The Evolution Timeline

1913: EOQ (Economic Order Quantity)

It started on paper. An engineer worked out a formula for the cheapest amount of stock to order. No computers, just math on a page trying to make smarter decisions.

The Information Pyramid

Under the Hood: Architecture

This section unveils how enterprise software is physically structured. By simulating the "Three-Tier Architecture," you will visualize the distinct roles of the Presentation (User Interface), Application (Business Logic), and Data (Database) tiers. This architectural pattern is crucial for maintaining security, scalability, and organized teamwork when building massive enterprise applications.

Three-Tier Flow

When you click a button, your request travels down through the layers, and the answer travels back up. This separation keeps the system secure and fast.

1.
Presentation Tier: What you see (browser, mobile app). Takes your input.
2.
Application Tier: The brain. Holds business rules and decides what to do.
3.
Data Tier: The database where facts are securely stored.
